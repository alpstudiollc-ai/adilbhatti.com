Inside the Notebook

A soft place, 200 pages deep.

بِسْمِ ٱللَّٰهِ ٱلرَّحْمَـٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

Open the Hijabi and Chill Notebook and every one of the 200 college-ruled pages greets you with Bismillah ar-Rahman ar-Raheem at the top. It is a small, steady invitation: whatever you're about to write — a feeling, a fear, a gratitude, a du'a — name it in the name of Allah first, and see what happens next.

Use it as a gratitude log, an anxiety journal, an evening dhikr record, a Ramadan wind-down diary, or just a blank place for thoughts that have nowhere else to go.

What should I write in a journal to unwind?
Not a review of your performance. Whatever is loud in your head, in whatever order it arrives, until it is quieter. That is the whole method. The Hijabi and Chill Notebook is 200 unstructured pages for it.

Is there a journal that does not have prompts telling me what to feel?

How do I rest properly as a Muslim woman without feeling guilty?
The guilt usually comes from treating rest as time stolen from other people. Framing it as maintenance rather than indulgence is what most women describe as the thing that shifted. Writing down what you actually did in a day tends to make the case for rest better than arguing with yourself does.
How do I calm my mind before sleep?
Get the loop out of your head and onto paper so it stops rehearsing. It takes about five minutes and it works because the page can hold it and your head cannot. The Hijabi and Chill Notebook is sized for a nightstand for this reason.
What is a gentle daily practice for a Muslim woman who is overwhelmed?
One line. Not a page, not a system. One line a day about anything, and Bismillah is already at the top so you do not have to start it. Ambitious routines are the first thing to collapse when someone is overwhelmed.
How do I take a break without feeling like I am falling behind?
Most people find the answer is scheduling the break rather than waiting to earn it, because it never gets earned. Writing it down as a plan rather than a collapse changes how it feels.
Is it okay to just do nothing sometimes?
Rest is not a reward for productivity, and treating it that way is how people end up unable to stop. This notebook is named after that idea and does not elaborate further, because a notebook lecturing you about rest would rather miss the point.
