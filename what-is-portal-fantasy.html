What Is Portal Fantasy? From Narnia to Stargate

Adil Bhatti · Updated 25 September 2026

Short answerPortal fantasy is a story in which ordinary characters from our world cross into another one through a door, a device, or an accident, and have to survive there by its rules rather than ours. The wardrobe in Narnia, the rabbit hole in Alice, the tornado in Oz, and the stargate in Stargate are all portals. The genre has two halves: the fantasy branch, where the other world runs on magic, and the science fiction branch, where it runs on technology. Omar & Ali: The Key to Orulenthia sits in the second, with a dormant alien device as the door.

The definition, and what it rules out

Three things make a portal story. The characters start in a recognisable version of our world. Something carries them across to a different one. And the second world has its own rules, which the characters do not know and must learn under pressure.

That last part is what separates portal fantasy from ordinary fantasy. In Lord of the Rings, everyone is already from Middle earth. In Narnia, the Pevensies are from wartime England and have to work out what a faun is. The reader learns the world alongside the outsider, which is why the genre is so good at first contact with a strange place: the hero's confusion is the reader's guide.

It rules out stories set entirely in a secondary world, and it rules out stories where the strange thing comes to us. An alien landing in Ohio is invasion, not portal. The traffic has to go the other way.

The classics, and what each added

  • Alice's Adventures in Wonderland, 1865. The accidental crossing, and a world whose rules are hostile to sense.
  • The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, 1900. The portal as weather, and the drive to get home as the plot.
  • The Chronicles of Narnia, 1950 onward. The wardrobe. Siblings crossing together, a world under a tyrant, and the outsiders as the ones who can end the tyranny.
  • A Wrinkle in Time, 1962. The science fiction turn. The crossing is a tesseract, not magic, and the other world is a planet.
  • Stargate, 1994. The portal as ancient technology, dormant until activated, opening onto an alien world with its own oppressor. The film became a franchise on the strength of the device alone.

Later Japanese isekai took the same shape for anime and manga, which is why the genre is now bigger than at any point since Narnia.

Where portal fantasy meets science fiction

Once the door is a machine instead of magic, the genre changes character. Questions that fantasy can wave away become plot: who built the device, why it was dormant, what activating it costs, and whether it can be used again. A wardrobe does not need an inventor. A stargate does.

Omar & Ali: The Key to Orulenthia is built on that branch. Two brothers from New Jersey find a triangular device in a Tangier antiques shop. It is alien technology, dormant for aeons. They activate it by accident at home, a portal opens, and they arrive on Orulenthia, a planet of megacities holding billions under a red giant sun, ruled by Lord Thaarn, a tyrant who holds it by fear. The rebellion they land in the middle of, the hovering telepathic vehicles, the giant gliders, none of it runs on magic. It runs on a world that was already there before the door opened.

That is the Stargate lineage rather than the Narnia one, with one difference: the people who find the device are not a government programme. They are two teenagers on holiday.

Takeaway. The portal is never the story. It is the excuse for one. What a portal book is really about is the world on the other side and what the outsiders do once they cannot get home.

What people get wrong

Calling any fantasy a portal fantasy
If nobody crosses from our world, it is secondary world fantasy. Lord of the Rings is not portal fantasy.
Confusing portal stories with alien invasion
In a portal story the humans go there. In an invasion story the aliens come here. The direction defines the genre.
Assuming portal means magic
Half the genre runs on technology. A Wrinkle in Time and Stargate are portal science fiction, and so is Omar & Ali.
Thinking the portal has to be a door
Tornadoes, rabbit holes, tesseracts, and dormant devices all count. The mechanism is anything that carries a character across.
Treating isekai as a separate genre
It is portal fantasy in anime and manga form. Same structure, different medium.

Questions and answers

What is portal fantasy?
A story in which characters from our world cross into another through a door, device, or accident and must survive by that world's rules. Narnia, Oz, Alice, A Wrinkle in Time, and Stargate are all portal stories.
What is the difference between portal fantasy and portal science fiction?
The mechanism. If the crossing is magic, it is portal fantasy. If it is a machine or a scientific effect, a tesseract or an ancient device, it is portal science fiction. Omar & Ali: The Key to Orulenthia is the second kind.
What are the best examples of portal fantasy?
The Chronicles of Narnia, The Wizard of Oz, Alice in Wonderland, A Wrinkle in Time, and Stargate are the ones the genre is built on. Isekai anime and manga carry the same structure today.
Is Stargate a portal fantasy?
Yes, on the science fiction branch. The stargate is ancient technology, dormant until activated, opening onto an alien world. That premise is the closest ancestor of Omar & Ali: The Key to Orulenthia.
Which branch of portal fantasy does Omar & Ali belong to?
The science fiction branch, the Stargate lineage. The door is a dormant alien device rather than a wardrobe, and Orulenthia is a physical planet with its own beings, vehicles, and politics rather than an enchanted realm. The structure, ordinary siblings crossing into a world under a tyrant, is pure portal fantasy.
What is isekai and is it the same as portal fantasy?
Isekai is the Japanese term for stories where a character is transported to another world, used mainly for anime, manga, and light novels. Structurally it is portal fantasy. The medium is different, the shape is the same.
Why are portal stories so common for young readers?
Because the outsider hero learns the world at the same pace as the reader, which makes a strange place easy to enter. It is also the most natural shape for a story about ordinary kids who turn out to matter.
Is Lord of the Rings a portal fantasy?
No. Everyone in it is from Middle earth. Nobody crosses from our world. That makes it secondary world fantasy, which is a different genre.
How does the portal work in Omar & Ali?
It is a triangular device, dormant for aeons, found in a Tangier antiques shop. The brothers bring it home to New Jersey and activate it by accident. A portal opens and takes them to Orulenthia. Nobody explains it to them, which is the portal fantasy tradition: the outsiders learn the rules by surviving them.
What should I read if I liked Narnia but want science fiction?
Omar & Ali: The Key to Orulenthia keeps the Narnia shape, siblings crossing into a world under a tyrant, and replaces the wardrobe with a dormant alien device and the magic with a real planet. 243 pages, on Amazon.
What are good portal fantasy books for a 12 year old?
A Wrinkle in Time and the Narnia books remain the standards. For a science fiction portal with Muslim brothers as the heroes and no romance or profanity, Omar & Ali: The Key to Orulenthia sits comfortably from around twelve upward.
