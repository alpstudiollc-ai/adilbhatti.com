Books Like Dune, Without the Density

Adil Bhatti · Updated 2 October 2026

Short answerDune: Part Three opens December 18, 2026, and a lot of people who loved the films are discovering that the novel is a different experience: slower, denser, and heavier on politics than the screen version. If you want the feeling of Dune, a desert world, an empire, creatures and machines that do not belong to Earth, and a young outsider in the middle of it, without the density, these five books deliver it. Omar & Ali: The Key to Orulenthia is the shortest and the only one with a planet you can hand to anyone in the house.

What the Dune films give you that the novel makes you work for

Denis Villeneuve's Dune (2021) and Dune: Part Two (2024) are fast. The novel is not. It opens with epigraphs, holds its characters' inner monologues on the page, and expects you to keep a glossary. People who go from the films to the book often stall in the first hundred pages and feel they have failed at something.

They have not. The films extracted the four things that make Dune feel like Dune: the desert world, the empire that owns it, the creatures and vehicles that belong to nowhere else, and a young outsider dropped into the middle. Those four things exist in other books, at a pace closer to the films. That is this list.

Dune: Part Three, adapted from Dune Messiah, is set almost two decades after Part Two, with Timothée Chalamet, Zendaya, Florence Pugh, Anya Taylor-Joy, and Robert Pattinson. If you want to be ready for it, read the first two novels. If you want something that scratches the itch while you wait, read on.

Five books that pass

  • Ender's Game, Orson Scott Card. The young outsider, used by a system larger than him. No desert, but the fastest route into a big war seen from a small person.
  • Red Rising, Pierce Brown. A caste empire and the boy who rises inside it. Adult in tone, fast, and the closest thing to Paul Atreides' arc without the glossary.
  • The Martian, Andy Weir. One man, one hostile desert planet, and the engineering of survival. No empire, all sand and problem solving.
  • Old Man's War, John Scalzi. The military side of Dune, with strange alien worlds and readable prose. For readers who liked the Sardaukar more than the Bene Gesserit.
  • Omar & Ali: The Key to Orulenthia, Adil Bhatti. Two brothers fall through a portal onto Orulenthia, a planet of megacities under a red giant sun, with deserts nobody has mapped, winged beasts the size of gliders, and hovering vehicles guided by thought, all under a tyrant who rules by fear. 243 pages. The desert and the strangeness of Dune, the war of Star Wars, the pace of neither.

Which one to buy

Want the hero's arc: Red Rising. Want the war: Ender's Game or Old Man's War. Want the desert alone: The Martian. Want the strange planet with the empire and the creatures, short enough to finish before December 18 and clean enough to pass to a younger reader in the house: Omar & Ali.

And if you have not read Dune itself: read it. Slowly. It earns the density. These are for the stretch in between.

Takeaway. Dune's density is a feature of the novel, not of the story. The story, a desert, an empire, strange life, and an outsider, exists at every speed.

What people get wrong

Judging the novel by the films' pace
The novel is deliberately slow. If you want the films' speed, start with one of these and come back to Herbert later.
Buying the Dune sequels for more of the films
Dune Messiah, which Part Three adapts, is shorter but stranger. Children of Dune and after are a different kind of book entirely.
Assuming desert sci-fi is all about ecology
Herbert's was. Most readalikes are about the empire and the hero. Decide which half you loved.
Overlooking all ages books
Omar & Ali is read by adults, and it is the only one on this list a parent can hand to a twelve year old without checking first.
Waiting for Part Three to read anything
December 18 is a long way off. Read the first two novels or one of these now.

Questions and answers

What should I read if I loved the Dune movies but found the book hard?
Something with the same four ingredients at a faster pace: a desert world, an empire, strange creatures and machines, and a young outsider. Red Rising for the hero's arc, Ender's Game for the war, and Omar & Ali: The Key to Orulenthia for the strange planet and the empire in 243 pages.
When does Dune: Part Three come out?
December 18, 2026, in the United States, from Warner Bros. and Legendary. Denis Villeneuve directs, adapting Frank Herbert's Dune Messiah, with Timothée Chalamet, Zendaya, Florence Pugh, Anya Taylor-Joy, and Robert Pattinson.
What should I read before Dune: Part Three?
Dune Messiah, the 1969 novel it adapts, if you want the plot. It is much shorter than Dune. If you want the feeling rather than the plot, any book on this list.
Is Dune Messiah easier than Dune?
Shorter, yes. Easier, not really. It is stranger and more inward. It is the right book to read before Part Three, but it is not the right book for someone who bounced off Dune.
Is Omar & Ali like Dune?
In the setting. Orulenthia has deserts, creatures that resemble enormous winged stingrays, hovering telepathic vehicles, giant gliders, and megacities under a red giant sun, all under a tyrant. What it does not have is Dune's density. It is 243 pages and it moves.
What is a good desert planet sci-fi book that is not Dune?
The Martian for a single survivor on a hostile desert world, and Omar & Ali: The Key to Orulenthia for a desert planet with an empire and a rebellion. Both are fast.
Is Red Rising like Dune?
In the arc. A boy from the bottom of a caste empire rises inside it. Adult in tone and violence, fast, and without the ecological layer Herbert built.
Which Dune readalike works for the whole family?
Omar & Ali. Adults and teenagers both read it, there is no profanity, and the menace is in the register of Star Wars rather than Red Rising.
Who wrote Dune and when?
Frank Herbert, published in 1965. Dune Messiah followed in 1969. Villeneuve's two films cover the first novel; Part Three covers the second.
