UAP vs UFO: What the Terms Mean and Why They Changed

Adil Bhatti · Updated 2 October 2026

Short answerUFO means unidentified flying object, a term the US Air Force coined in the early 1950s. UAP originally meant unidentified aerial phenomena and was adopted by the Pentagon and Congress in the late 2010s to shed the cultural baggage of UFO and to include things that are not objects and may not be flying. In 2022 the US government widened it again to unidentified anomalous phenomena so it could cover objects in space and underwater as well as in the air. Neither word means alien. Both mean: observed, recorded, and not yet explained.

What each term actually says

UFO is a claim about a thing. Unidentified, flying, object: something solid, in the air, that nobody has named. The word carries seventy years of films, hoaxes, and tabloid covers, which is the reason official bodies stopped using it.

UAP is a claim about an observation. Unidentified anomalous phenomena: something was detected, by eye or by sensor, and it does not fit a known category. It may be an object, a light, a radar return, or a sensor artefact. It may be in the air, in orbit, or in the sea. The word is deliberately wider and deliberately colder.

The move from one to the other is not cosmetic. It changes what counts, who is allowed to report it without ridicule, and which agency takes the file.

When the switch happened, and why

UFO entered official use with the Air Force's Project Blue Book era in the 1950s and stayed there through the Cold War. By the 2010s it had become a word a Navy pilot could not put in a report without consequences for his career. That silence was the problem the new term was meant to solve.

UAP, as unidentified aerial phenomena, was adopted by the Pentagon's UAP Task Force in 2020, used by Congress in the 2021 and 2022 defence bills, and became the standard term when AARO, the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, was created in 2022. AARO's name carried the second change: aerial became anomalous, so the office could take reports from space and sea as well as sky. The 2023 Congressional hearings used UAP throughout. The Pentagon's PURSUE file releases in 2026 use it in every document.

Takeaway. UFO is the word the history was written in. UAP is the word the files are written in. Neither one means alien, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling the conclusion before the evidence.

Who says what today

Government and military: UAP, without exception, in anything official. Pilots and intelligence officers: UAP in a report, often UFO in conversation. Journalists: both, usually UAP in the headline and UFO in the body. The public and most search engines: still UFO, by a wide margin. Researchers and authors: increasingly both in the same sentence, because the history was written under one word and the present is being written under the other.

That split is why a serious book on the subject needs both words in its title. The ancient and historic record is a UFO record. The Congressional record is a UAP record. They are the same subject.

What people get wrong

Treating UAP as a euphemism for UFO
It is wider, not softer. It covers things that are not objects and are not in the air.
Assuming the term change was a cover up
It was the opposite. The old word stopped people reporting. The new one was meant to get reports filed.
Thinking UAP means the government admitted aliens
It means the government admitted it records things it cannot identify. Those are different admissions.
Using UAP for the historic cases
The 1942 Battle of Los Angeles and Roswell were UFO cases in their own time. Renaming them backwards loses the context.
Dropping UFO entirely
Most people still search for it. A book or a guide that only says UAP is invisible to the people looking for it.

Questions and answers

What does UAP stand for?
Unidentified anomalous phenomena. It originally stood for unidentified aerial phenomena and was widened in 2022 when the Pentagon created AARO, so the term could cover detections in space and underwater as well as in the air.
What is the difference between UFO and UAP?
UFO describes a thing: an unidentified flying object. UAP describes an observation: an unidentified anomalous phenomenon, which may be an object, a light, a sensor return, or something in orbit or at sea. UAP is wider, newer, and carries none of the cultural baggage.
Why did the government stop saying UFO?
Because the word had become a career risk. Military pilots would not put it in a report. The Pentagon adopted UAP around 2020 so that detections could be filed and investigated without ridicule.
Does UAP mean aliens?
No. It means something was observed and has not been explained. The same is true of UFO. Both words describe the state of the evidence, not its cause.
When did UAP change from aerial to anomalous?
In 2022, with the creation of the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, AARO. The office's remit covered air, space, and sea, so the term widened to match.
Is UAP the official term now?
Yes. The Pentagon, Congress, AARO, and the 2026 PURSUE file releases all use UAP. UFO does not appear in official US government language.
Should I say UFO or UAP?
Both, depending on who you are talking to. UAP if you are reading or quoting official documents. UFO if you want to be understood by most people. A good book on the subject uses both.
What is AARO?
The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, the Pentagon office created in 2022 to investigate UAP reports across air, space, and sea. Its documents appear in the PURSUE releases.
Were the historic cases UFOs or UAP?
UFOs, in the language of their time. The 1942 Battle of Los Angeles, Roswell, Rendlesham Forest, and the Phoenix Lights were all reported and investigated under the old word. Ancient Aliens to Modern UFOs keeps that distinction, which is why both words are in the title.
