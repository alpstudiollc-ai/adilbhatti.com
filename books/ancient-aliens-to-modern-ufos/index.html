Right Now, 2026

The files are opening. This is the map.

Since May 2026 the Pentagon has been publishing declassified UAP records to the public on a rolling basis, and the releases have not stopped.

The program is called PURSUE, the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters. The first release went online on May 8 with more than 160 files. Three more followed, on May 22, June 12, and July 10, drawing on records from the Pentagon, NASA, the CIA, the FBI, and the Department of Energy. Officials have confirmed the next batch is already in preparation. The archive is public and free, and you can read the files yourself at the Pentagon's UAP archive rather than through anyone's summary of them.

What is coming out is not one clean revelation. It is decades of paperwork. Project Sign reports from 1948. A 1949 conference transcript on green fireballs over Los Alamos, attended by scientists who had worked on the Manhattan Project. A Project Blue Book advisory review from the mid sixties. NASA imagery from a 1996 shuttle mission. A silent, diamond shaped object recorded over the Pantex nuclear weapons facility in Texas in 2015, with security officers unable to identify any propulsion system while watching it through binoculars. A Navy aviator describing an object with flight characteristics unlike anything he had seen in twenty eight years of service.

This book was published in December 2025, roughly five months before the first PURSUE file went online. It does not cover those specific documents and does not claim to. Read on its own, a declassified file is a blurry object and a redacted paragraph. Read against a century of context, it becomes part of a pattern. That context is what this book is for.

The Stakes

This is bigger than the sky.

The biggest story in human history is unfolding right now, and its implications reach far beyond anything hovering above us.

If the propulsion technology behind credible UFO and UAP sightings is genuine, then aerospace itself operates on principles fundamentally different from anything the modern global economy is built on. That technology, by every credible description, appears to draw on energy sources that are effectively free and boundlessly available, outside the framework of fossil fuels and petroleum-driven capitalism that currently defines national economies, geopolitical power, and trillions of dollars of industrial infrastructure. Genuine disclosure therefore carries consequences that extend directly into energy policy, market stability, and the incentives that have historically governed what research is funded, what is published, and what is quietly shelved.

The implications do not end at the economic. Confirmation of non-human intelligence visiting Earth, across millennia, would force a reconsideration of foundational assumptions in nearly every major religious tradition, and of the dominant secular narrative about humanity's origins, uniqueness, and place in the universe. It is the kind of question that societies rarely encounter more than once in their history.

This book takes that full picture seriously, and then walks the evidence, from the earliest recorded encounters to the testimony being entered into the Congressional record today.
