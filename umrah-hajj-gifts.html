Umrah and Hajj Gifts: Before They Go and When They Return

Adil Bhatti · Updated 16 October 2026

Short answerThere are two gift moments around a pilgrimage and they want different things. Before they go, the gift should be useful on the journey: light, practical, and somewhere to keep the du'a list, because everyone they know will ask to be remembered at the Kaaba. When they return, the gift should mark what they did without being heavy, because a pilgrim comes home with Zamzam and dates for everyone else and very little for themselves. An Islamic notebook with Bismillah on every page works at both moments. Before, it is the du'a list and the trip journal. After, it is where the resolutions made at Arafah go so they survive the first month home. Hajj 2027 is expected around May 14 to 19, with the Day of Arafah about May 15, subject to the moon. Umrah runs all year and peaks in Ramadan, which begins around February 8, 2027.

Before they go: what a pilgrim actually needs

Two weeks before departure, a pilgrim starts collecting names. Parents, cousins, the neighbor, the colleague who heard about the trip. Every one of them asks the same thing: make du'a for me there. By the airport the list is forty names long and lives on three scraps of paper and a phone that will be in a locker at the Haram.

That is the gift. A notebook, given two weeks out, with page one for the names. The rest of the pages hold the trip itself: the first sight of the Kaaba, the du'a made at Arafah, the things noticed that nobody tells you about. Hardcover, because it goes in a suitcase and comes back out. For a man, Pray Du'a Shawarma Repeat, whose title is the pilgrim's actual day. For a woman, Hijabi 'til I Die if she is recommitting, Hijabi Queen if the trip is a milestone she has earned.

Keep everything else light. Luggage allowances are real, ihram has no pockets, and anything heavy stays in the hotel. The notebook weighs 12.8 ounces.

When they return: the welcome back gift

A returning pilgrim walks into a week of visitors. By custom they give, not receive: Zamzam water, dates, prayer beads, a scarf from Madinah. Most come home having bought something for everyone and nothing for themselves. The gift that lands marks what they did, and gives the change somewhere to live.

Everyone who stands at Arafah makes resolutions. Most of them are gone by the second Friday home. A notebook with Bismillah on every page is where those resolutions survive: the new routine, the debts settled, the people forgiven. Match the cover to the person:

  • A mother who finally went: Hijabi Queen. The trip was hers.
  • A man who saved for years to go: Salah, Shawarma, Success!! A goal reached, and the next one.
  • Someone who went after a hard year: Hijabi and Unbreakable, quietly.
  • Someone who came home exhausted: Hijabi and Chill. Hajj is physically brutal. Rest is allowed.
  • A group of friends who went together: Hijabi Gang, Where My Girls At? One each.
  • The welcome dinner itself: It's Shawarma Time Ya Salaam, for the host who is feeding thirty people the night the pilgrim lands.
  • A business owner who closed the shop to go: Halal Hustler. Reopening with a clean page.

Umrah or Hajj: does the gift change?

A little. Umrah is shorter, can be done any time of year outside the Hajj days, and many people do it more than once, so the gift can be lighter. Paperback is fine for a second or third Umrah. Ramadan Umrah is the most sought after, and Ramadan 2027 begins around February 8, so gifts for a Ramadan trip need to be in hand by late January.

Hajj is once in a lifetime for most people and comes with a title they keep. Give hardcover. Hajj 2027 is expected around May 14 to 19, with the Day of Arafah about May 15, all subject to the moon sighting in Saudi Arabia. Pilgrims leave one to three weeks before that, so a before-they-go gift is an April gift. Order two weeks ahead of when you need it.

Takeaway. Before they go, give the du'a list. When they return, give the resolutions somewhere to live. Same notebook, two different jobs, and the cover is chosen by the person, not the trip.

What people get wrong

Giving the gift at the airport
Too late for the du'a list, which is the whole point of giving it beforehand. Two weeks out.
Giving something heavy before travel
Luggage limits, ihram with no pockets, and a hotel room they barely see. Light or nothing.
Giving the returnee something for the guests
They already bought for the guests. The gift is for them.
Assuming Umrah does not need a gift
It is still a journey to the Kaaba. Smaller gift, same thought. Paperback is fine.
Buying paperback for a Hajj return
Hardcover. It is the one trip most people make once.
Giving a returning pilgrim a book of instructions
They just did the course. Give them somewhere to write what they learned instead.

Questions and answers

What is a good gift for someone going on Umrah?
Something light, useful on the trip, and given two weeks before departure: a notebook for the du'a list and the journey. For a man, Pray Du'a Shawarma Repeat. For a woman, Hijabi 'til I Die or Hijabi Queen. Paperback is fine for Umrah, especially a repeat trip.
What is a good gift for someone going to Hajj?
A hardcover notebook with Bismillah on every page, given two to three weeks before they leave, with page one for the names of everyone who asked to be remembered. Pray Du'a Shawarma Repeat for a man, Hijabi Queen or Hijabi 'til I Die for a woman. Keep everything else light because of luggage limits.
What do you give someone when they return from Hajj?
Something that marks what they did and asks nothing of them, because they come home having given gifts to everyone else. A hardcover Islamic notebook where the resolutions made at Arafah can live. Match the cover to the person: Hijabi Queen for a mother, Salah, Shawarma, Success!! for a man who saved for years, Hijabi and Unbreakable for someone who went after a hard year.
What do you give someone returning from Umrah?
A smaller version of the Hajj return gift. A paperback notebook is right, in the cover that fits them. If they went in Ramadan, Hijabi and Chill or Pray Du'a Shawarma Repeat, because they come home into Eid and need somewhere to land.
Should you give the gift before they leave or after they come back?
Both moments are real and want different things. Before, the gift is practical and goes in the suitcase. After, the gift is a marker and stays at home. If you can only do one, give it before, because the du'a list is the thing they will actually use at the Kaaba.
Why does a pilgrim need a notebook?
For the du'a list. Dozens of people ask to be remembered, and the list ends up on scraps of paper and a phone that is locked away at the Haram. A notebook holds the names, then the trip, then the resolutions. Bismillah at the top of every page means the first thing written each day is His name.
When is Hajj in 2027?
Expected around May 14 to 19, 2027, with the Day of Arafah about Saturday, May 15. The exact dates depend on the sighting of the Dhul Hijjah moon in Saudi Arabia and are confirmed a few weeks before. Pilgrims typically leave one to three weeks early, so gifts for someone going should be in hand by late April.
When is the best time to go for Umrah?
Any time outside the Hajj days. Ramadan Umrah is the most sought after and the most crowded; Ramadan 2027 begins around February 8. Winter months are cooler and quieter. A gift for a Ramadan Umrah should be given by late January.
Which notebook suits a mother returning from Hajj?
Hijabi Queen, in hardcover. The trip was hers, often after decades of putting everyone else first, and the cover says so without a speech.
Which notebook suits a man who saved for years to go?
Salah, Shawarma, Success!! It is the goal notebook. He reached one, and the pages after are for the next.
Is a shawarma themed notebook appropriate as a Hajj gift?
The covers are playful; the pages are not. Every one of the 200 pages opens with Bismillah ar-Rahman ar-Raheem in Arabic calligraphy. Give it at the welcome dinner or two weeks before departure, not in the Haram. For the journey itself, Pray Du'a Shawarma Repeat is the obvious one, because the title is the pilgrim's actual day.
What do returning pilgrims traditionally give to visitors?
Zamzam water, dates from Madinah, prayer beads, scarves, and small items bought near the Haram. That custom is why the pilgrim often comes home with nothing for themselves, and why the gift they receive should be for them alone.
